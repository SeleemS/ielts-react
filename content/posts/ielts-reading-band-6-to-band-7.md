  <p>Many candidates stall at Band 6 in <strong>IELTS Reading</strong> not because they cannot understand the passages, but because they run out of time, guess on the trickiest question types, and lose a mark or two to careless transfer. The good news is that the jump to Band 7 is smaller than it feels. This guide shows you exactly what separates the two bands and the habits that close the gap.</p>

  <h2>What Band 6 and Band 7 Actually Mean</h2>
  <p>The Reading test has <strong>40 questions</strong> answered in <strong>60 minutes</strong>, and — unlike Listening — there is <strong>no extra time to transfer answers</strong>. Your raw score out of 40 is converted to a band using published indicative boundaries. In <strong>Academic Reading</strong>, roughly <strong>23 correct gives Band 6 and around 30 gives Band 7</strong>. So the jump you are chasing is only about <strong>seven extra correct answers</strong> — usually ones you are currently losing to slow pacing or avoidable question-type mistakes, not to genuinely impossible text.</p>
  <p>One crucial difference: <strong>General Training Reading is scaled more strictly</strong> because its passages are easier. There, Band 6 is around 27 correct and Band 7 around 34. Know which test you are sitting, and check how a Reading band feeds into your overall result with our free <a href="/band-calculator">IELTS band score calculator</a>.</p>

  <h2>Fix Your Timing First</h2>
  <p>The single most common Band 6 trap is spending too long on the first passage and arriving at Passage 3 — usually the hardest — with only ten minutes left. The fix is a simple, non-negotiable rule:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Spend about 20 minutes on each passage</strong>, including reading, answering, and writing onto the answer sheet.</li>
    <li><strong>Never leave a question blank.</strong> There is no penalty for a wrong answer, so a guess costs nothing and sometimes scores.</li>
    <li><strong>If a question resists you after a minute, mark it and move on.</strong> An easy answer two questions later is worth exactly the same mark, so protect the easy ones.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Band 7 candidates treat the clock as an opponent and finish every passage. Band 6 candidates let one hard passage swallow the marks that were sitting easily in the next.</p>

  <h2>Read the Question Type, Not Just the Question</h2>
  <p>Each <a href="/readingquestion">IELTS Reading question type</a> rewards a different approach, and Band 7 candidates adjust deliberately rather than reading everything the same way.</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>True/False/Not Given</strong> and <strong>Yes/No/Not Given</strong> are where most Band 6 marks leak away. The rule is strict: <em>Not Given</em> means the passage neither confirms nor contradicts the statement — do not use outside knowledge or assumptions. If you cannot point to text that supports or opposes it, it is Not Given. Practise this deliberately on our <a href="/reading/true-false-not-given">True/False/Not Given</a> hub.</li>
    <li><strong>Matching Headings</strong> tests the main idea of each paragraph, not a matching keyword. Read the paragraph, summarise it in your own words, then find the heading that fits — never match on a single repeated word.</li>
    <li><strong>Gap-fill and completion tasks</strong> demand the exact word from the passage in the exact form. Check the word limit ("no more than two words") and copy spelling precisely.</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>Track Keywords and Their Synonyms</h2>
  <p>IELTS almost never uses the exact wording of the question in the passage. Band 6 readers scan for the same word and miss the answer because the text paraphrased it. Band 7 readers scan for <strong>meaning</strong>. When you read a question, quickly note the likely synonyms before you scan:</p>
  <ul>
    <li>"children" may appear as <em>youngsters</em>, <em>minors</em>, or <em>the young</em>.</li>
    <li>"reduce" may appear as <em>cut</em>, <em>fall</em>, <em>decline</em>, or <em>lower</em>.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Skimming for structure and scanning for these synonyms is a trainable skill — see our guide to <a href="/blog/ielts-reading-skimming-and-scanning">skimming and scanning</a> for a step-by-step method.</p>

  <h2>Protect Every Mark on Transfer</h2>
  <p>Because there is no extra transfer time, Band 6 candidates rush the answer sheet and lose marks to three predictable errors:</p>
  <ol>
    <li><strong>Spelling.</strong> A correctly located answer spelled wrong scores zero. Copy exactly from the text.</li>
    <li><strong>Word form.</strong> If the answer is a plural and you write the singular, it is wrong. Match number and tense to what the question requires.</li>
    <li><strong>Wrong line.</strong> When you skip a question, make sure you skip the matching line on the answer sheet too — one misaligned answer can push the next five out of place.</li>
  </ol>

  <h2>A Passage-by-Passage Plan</h2>
  <p>Put it together in a routine you repeat for all three passages:</p>
  <ol>
    <li><strong>Skim the passage for 2-3 minutes</strong> to grasp its structure and the role of each paragraph.</li>
    <li><strong>Read the questions and note synonyms</strong> before scanning back into the text.</li>
    <li><strong>Answer in question order where possible</strong>, writing directly onto the answer sheet as you confirm each one.</li>
    <li><strong>Guess any remaining blanks</strong> before your 20 minutes are up, then move on without looking back.</li>
  </ol>

  <h2>The Bottom Line</h2>
  <p>Band 7 in IELTS Reading is not about reading faster or knowing more words — it is about losing fewer marks. Fix your pacing so Passage 3 gets a fair share of the clock, treat Not Given as a strict category, scan for meaning rather than matching words, and transfer answers with care. Close those leaks and the seven-answer gap closes with them. When you are ready, drill each question type on our <a href="/readingquestion">Reading practice hub</a> and track your progress toward a confident Band 7.</p>
