  <p>In <strong>IELTS Academic Writing Task 1</strong> you are given a visual, a line graph, bar chart, pie chart, table, map, or process diagram, and 20 minutes to describe it in at least 150 words. Many candidates treat it as a warm-up and rush it, but Task 1 counts toward your Writing band, and a weak report can drag down an otherwise strong Task 2. The good news is that Task 1 is highly formulaic: once you learn a reliable structure and a bank of trend language, you can score well on almost any chart you are given.</p>

  <h2>How Task 1 Is Marked</h2>
  <p>Your report is assessed on four equally weighted criteria:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Task Achievement:</strong> Do you cover the key features, include an overview, and report data accurately?</li>
    <li><strong>Coherence and Cohesion:</strong> Is the report logically organised and clearly linked?</li>
    <li><strong>Lexical Resource:</strong> Do you use a range of vocabulary to describe data and trends?</li>
    <li><strong>Grammatical Range and Accuracy:</strong> Do you use varied structures with few errors?</li>
  </ul>
  <p>The single most common reason candidates stay stuck at Band 5 or 6 is a missing or vague <strong>overview</strong>. It is the most important sentence in the whole report.</p>

  <h2>A Four-Paragraph Structure That Always Works</h2>

  <h3>1. Introduction (1 sentence)</h3>
  <p>Paraphrase the question. Do not copy it, change the wording and sentence structure. If the prompt says "The chart below shows the number of visitors to three museums," you might write: "The bar chart illustrates how many people visited three different museums over a ten-year period." Swap <em>shows</em> for <em>illustrates</em>, <em>depicts</em>, or <em>presents</em>, and reword the rest.</p>

  <h3>2. Overview (2 sentences)</h3>
  <p>State the two or three most important features without any specific numbers. This is where you show the examiner you can see the big picture: the highest and lowest values, the overall trend, or the most striking contrast. Begin with <em>Overall</em> to signal it clearly. For example: "Overall, visitor numbers rose across all three museums, with the largest increase seen at the science museum, which overtook the others by the final year."</p>

  <h3>3 and 4. Two Body Paragraphs (details)</h3>
  <p>Group the data logically and report specific figures to support your overview. Do not describe every single data point, select and compare the significant ones. A common grouping is highest values in one paragraph and lowest in another, or one time period versus another. Always include units and use accurate numbers straight from the chart.</p>

  <h2>Language for Describing Trends</h2>
  <p>Examiners reward a range of vocabulary for movement and comparison. Build a small, reliable bank:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Upward:</strong> rose, increased, climbed, surged, grew, went up</li>
    <li><strong>Downward:</strong> fell, decreased, declined, dropped, plummeted, dipped</li>
    <li><strong>No change:</strong> remained stable, stayed constant, levelled off, plateaued</li>
    <li><strong>Degree:</strong> sharply, dramatically, steadily, gradually, slightly, marginally</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Vary your grammar too. You can use a verb with an adverb ("sales increased sharply") or an adjective with a noun ("there was a sharp increase in sales"). Alternating between the two shows grammatical range and helps you avoid repetition.</p>

  <h2>Special Cases: Maps and Processes</h2>
  <p>If you get a <strong>map</strong> (changes to a place over time), use the passive voice and language of location and change: "a car park was built to the north," "the forest was replaced by housing." If you get a <strong>process diagram</strong>, describe the stages in order using sequencers (<em>first, next, after that, finally</em>) and the passive voice ("the beans are roasted, then ground"). Neither of these needs an overview of trends, instead summarise the number of stages or the overall direction of change.</p>

  <h2>Common Mistakes to Avoid</h2>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Giving opinions.</strong> Task 1 is a factual report. Never explain why a trend happened or say whether it is good, there are no marks for opinion here.</li>
    <li><strong>Listing every number.</strong> Selective comparison scores higher than an exhaustive data dump.</li>
    <li><strong>Writing under 150 words.</strong> You lose marks automatically. Aim for roughly 160 to 190.</li>
    <li><strong>Spending too long.</strong> Keep Task 1 to 20 minutes so you protect the 40 minutes Task 2 deserves.</li>
  </ul>
