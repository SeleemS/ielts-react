  <p>Search for "IELTS database" and you will find two very different things wearing the same name. The first is what many searchers are secretly hoping for: a collection of real, leaked or "recalled" exam questions. The second is what actually exists and actually helps: a large, organised bank of <strong>original practice questions</strong> built to match the exam's format, difficulty and topic range. This article is honest about the first and practical about the second — including exactly how to use a question bank so it raises your band rather than your confidence alone.</p>

  <h2>First, the honest part: real exam questions are not available</h2>
  <p>Genuine IELTS test papers are confidential. The exam boards own them, rotate them under strict security, and never release live questions to the public. Anyone claiming to sell or share "real past papers" is offering one of three things: fabricated material dressed up as authentic, secondhand "memories" posted by test-takers (which are incomplete, inaccurate and missing the parts that matter — the exact wording, options and marking), or in the worst case genuinely stolen content, which has led to cancelled results and bans for candidates involved with it. Every question on IELTS-Bank is <strong>original material written for practice</strong> — modelled on the exam's published format and style, never copied from any live test. We consider that a feature: the skills the exam measures are exactly the thing practice can build, and no shortcut around them survives contact with test day.</p>

  <h2>Why chasing "real questions" fails even when it seems to work</h2>
  <p>Suppose a recalled topic did reappear on your test day. What would you actually have? Reading and Listening answers depend on the precise text, recording and option wording — none of which recalls preserve. A memorised Task 2 essay on a familiar topic routinely fails Task Response because the live question's angle differs from the remembered one, and examiners are trained to spot rehearsed content in both Writing and Speaking. The exam is deliberately built so that familiarity with a <em>topic</em> confers almost nothing without the underlying skills: paraphrase recognition, evidence location, structured argument, fluent delivery. Those skills are trainable. Question-hunting is not training.</p>

  <h2>What a legitimate question bank is for</h2>
  <p>A well-built practice database earns its place in your preparation through structure, not secrets:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Format fluency.</strong> IELTS uses a stable set of question types — around a dozen in Reading, a handful in Listening, fixed task shapes in Writing and Speaking. A bank organised by type lets you meet every one before the exam does, so test day contains no format surprises at all.</li>
    <li><strong>Targeted repetition.</strong> The decisive advantage of a filterable bank: when you discover that matching headings or map labelling costs you marks, you can drill twenty examples of exactly that type — something no linear practice book allows.</li>
    <li><strong>Realistic topic exposure.</strong> The exam draws on recurring theme families — education, technology, environment, health, work. Practising original questions across those themes builds the vocabulary and ideas that transfer to whatever variation you meet.</li>
    <li><strong>Measurement.</strong> Enough material to take repeated timed tests without repeating questions, which is what makes your practice scores meaningful evidence rather than memory effects.</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>How to use a question bank properly</h2>
  <ol>
    <li><strong>Establish a baseline.</strong> One full timed test — our <a href="/mock-test">mock test</a> runs the complete experience — to find your current band and, more importantly, your per-skill and per-question-type weak spots. The <a href="/band-estimator">band estimator</a> gives a faster snapshot if you are earlier in the process.</li>
    <li><strong>Drill the diagnosis, not the syllabus.</strong> Spend most of your practice time on your two or three weakest question types. Do sets untimed first, with full review; the review — finding the exact line or audio moment that proves each answer — is where the improvement lives.</li>
    <li><strong>Classify your errors.</strong> "Wrong answer" is not a diagnosis. Missed paraphrase, broke the word limit, fell for a rejected option, lost my place — named errors stop repeating; unnamed ones do not.</li>
    <li><strong>Reintegrate under time.</strong> Skills drilled in isolation must survive exam pressure, so fold each repaired type back into full timed sections in the <a href="/readingquestion">Reading</a> and <a href="/listeningquestion">Listening</a> banks, and practise Writing prompts against the clock with objective scoring from the <a href="/ielts-writing-checker">writing checker</a>.</li>
    <li><strong>Retest and compare.</strong> A fresh timed test every couple of weeks, on unseen material, tells you whether the plan is working — accuracy by question type, time used, and error categories, not just the headline score.</li>
  </ol>

  <h2>The false-confidence trap</h2>
  <p>The subtle danger of any large practice collection is mistaking familiarity for readiness. Scoring well on your third attempt at a passage you half-remember proves nothing; recognising topics is not the same as handling unseen questions under time. Protect the integrity of your measurements: keep some of the bank unseen for testing, always review misses on practice material, and treat only fresh-material timed scores as evidence of your level. Convert those raw scores with the <a href="/band-calculator">band score calculator</a> to track your genuine trajectory.</p>

  <h2>The bottom line</h2>
  <p>There is no public database of real IELTS questions, and you do not need one. The exam's formats are published, its topic families are stable, and its skills are trainable — which means a large bank of original, well-made practice questions plus a disciplined review loop covers everything that actually determines your band. That is what this site is: original questions across <a href="/readingquestion">Reading</a>, <a href="/listeningquestion">Listening</a>, <a href="/speakingquestion">Speaking</a> and <a href="/writingquestion">Writing</a>, organised so you can find your weakness and fix it. Spend your energy there, not on the hunt for papers that do not exist.</p>
